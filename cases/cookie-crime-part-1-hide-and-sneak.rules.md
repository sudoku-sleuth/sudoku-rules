Dynamic fog: The grid is partially covered in fog. Placing correct digits might clear some fog from its surrounding cells, possibly revealing more clues. Sometimes extra cells can be revealed as well.

Chaos Construction: Each row, column, and region contains the digits 1-9 exactly once each. A region is an orthogonally connected collection of cells. The shape and position of the regions have to be determined with the help of the following clues:

Circles: There are 9 blue circles (with thicker outline) in the grid. Each blue circle belongs to a different region. 
Additionally there are black circles (with thinner outline). These can be in any region and a region can have more than 1 black circle.

The digit in a circle (blue as well as black) counts the number of cells that the circle sees from its own region in the row and column up to the first border including itself. 

Small magenta arrows count the number of region borders seen in the direction of the small arrow. If a cell has more than arrow, they see the same amount of borders in all pointed directions (not the sum of borders). The outer edges of the grid do not count as borders.

Digits separated by a white dot are consecutive. Digits separated by a black dot are in a 1:2 ratio. Not all possible dots are given. (There is no negative constraint.)
